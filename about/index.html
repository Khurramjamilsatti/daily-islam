Daily Islam

About Daily Islam

Daily Islam is a free website and a free phone app for people who want the Quran, hadith, prayer times, and a few daily tools in one calm place. It is published by Muhammad Khurram Jamil. You can write to khurram.jamilsatti@gmail.com.

What you can read

The Quran is shown in the Indo-Pak script. You can read it with English and Urdu translations, as Arabic only, or word by word, with a meaning under each word. Every one of those texts can be opened surah by surah or juz by juz. A juz can cross from one surah into the next. Ruku, manzil, and the quarter marks are shown when you are reading a juz. They stay off the page when you are reading a single surah.

Hadith is the text of six books: Sahih al-Bukhari, Sahih Muslim, Sunan Abi Dawud, Jami at-Tirmidhi, Sunan an-Nasa’i, and Sunan Ibn Majah. You can open a book, then a chapter, and you can search by hadith number. Urdu is included where that translation is part of the source used here. An empty Urdu line is labeled as unavailable rather than filled with a different narration.

Seerah books open in a reader when the file is available. Short guides cover salah, wudu, ghusl, the janazah prayer, Hajj and Umrah, qurbani, the pillars, the six kalimas, and a glossary of terms.

Prayer, nasheeds, and the halal check

Prayer times are calculated on the device from the location you allow and from the calculation method you pick. They are a help for the day, not a replacement for your local mosque. The qibla direction uses the phone compass. The Hijri date is calculated, so moon sighting can still shift Ramadan or the Eids by a day.

Nasheeds are streamed for listening. Arabic tracks are vocal. The Urdu list includes public recordings so there is a real library to browse. Tracks with a war theme are not included.

Scan Halal sends a barcode to a public product database and then checks the listed ingredients on your device. Use it as a reading aid. It is not a fatwa, and a missing product does not mean the food is halal. Check the package yourself.

The website and the phone apps

The website and the Android and iOS apps share the same reading. The phone apps can ask for location, notifications, and the camera, and they show Google AdMob ads. This website does not ask you to sign in, and it does not keep an account. Notes and favorites on a phone stay on that phone.

The website is supported by Google AdSense. One ad sits in the home page and one sits at the top of a detail page, inside the flow of the page. No other ad network is loaded.

If you want the longer explanation of how to move through the Quran, the hadith books, and the prayer page, read the reading guide. Questions about the site itself go to the contact page.
