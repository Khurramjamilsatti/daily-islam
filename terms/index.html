Quran, hadith, and books

Quran text in the app follows the Indo-Pak script. Translations include English and, where it is available, Urdu. Hadith is drawn from the six books: Sahih al-Bukhari, Sahih Muslim, Sunan Abi Dawud, Jami at-Tirmidhi, Sunan an-Nasa’i, and Sunan Ibn Majah.

Some hadith still have no Urdu text in the app. Where that happens, the app says the Urdu translation is not available. That gap is a limit of the published source, not a hidden passage.

Seerah books open inside the reader when a file is available. A book that fails to load can be opened again later. Daily Islam does not promise that every title will remain online forever, because some files are hosted outside the app.

Prayer times and the Hijri date

Prayer times are calculated on the device from your location and from the method and madhab you choose. They are a guide for the day. Local mosque announcements and moon-sighting decisions can differ by a few minutes or, for the calendar, by a day.

The Hijri date is calculated. Moon sighting can shift Ramadan and the two Eids by a day. Confirm the start and end of Ramadan, and the Eid days, with your local community.

Nasheeds

Nasheeds are streamed for listening inside Daily Islam. Arabic tracks are vocal. The Urdu list includes public recordings so the library can be large enough to browse. Tracks with a war theme are not included.

Playback depends on the network. A track that does not start can be tried again. Daily Islam does not promise that every recording will stay available, because the audio is served from outside hosts.

Halal scan

Scan Halal looks up a barcode and checks the listed ingredients. The result is a reading aid. It is not a fatwa, and it is not a certificate. Ingredients lists can be incomplete, out of date, or different from the package in your hand.

Check the package yourself, and ask a scholar when a product matters for your worship. A missing product, or a product with no ingredients, means the lookup could not decide. It does not mean the product is halal.

Qibla, notifications, and the flashlight

The qibla direction uses the phone’s compass and location. Metal, cases, and indoor walls can pull the compass off. Calibrate the compass and confirm the direction before you rely on it.

Prayer reminders are local notifications on the phone. They fire only if you allow notifications and if the app is allowed to run them. The flashlight control uses the phone lamp for wudu or for finding a page. It is not available as a website feature.
