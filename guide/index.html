Reading guide

This page explains how to use Daily Islam. The site is free. Start from the home page, then open the Quran, a hadith book, or the prayer times. Nothing here requires an account.

Reading the Quran

Choose Quran in the menu. You can open the text with English and Urdu, the Arabic text alone, or a word-by-word view. Recitation is separate: full surah recordings by Mishary Rashid Alafasy, Raad Al-Kurdi, Abdul Rahman Al-Sudais, and Saad Al-Ghamdi.

Each of the three text views has a Surah and a Juz switch. Surah reading shows that surah from the first ayah, without ruku, manzil, or quarter marks. Juz reading shows only the ayahs that belong to that juz. A juz can begin in the middle of a surah and continue into the next one. On a juz page you will see the manzil when an ayah starts one, the quarter mark above an ayah, and the ruku mark at the end of each ruku.

The Arabic is the Indo-Pak script. Word by word places a short meaning under each word, in English or in Urdu. Use it to follow a verse slowly. It is a learning aid beside the full translation, not a replacement for it.

Reading hadith

Hadith Books opens the six books: Sahih al-Bukhari, Sahih Muslim, Sunan Abi Dawud, Jami at-Tirmidhi, Sunan an-Nasa’i, and Sunan Ibn Majah. Pick a book, then a chapter. You can also search by the hadith number instead of scrolling the whole book.

Arabic is shown for each hadith. Urdu is shown when that narration has an Urdu translation in the source used by Daily Islam. If the Urdu is not available, the page says so. It does not copy a neighbouring hadith’s translation into the gap. You can share a hadith from the share icon. The text goes to the share sheet on your device.

Prayer times

Open Prayer for today’s times. The calculation uses your location and the method and madhab you select, and it runs on your device. Allow location if you want times for where you are. If you refuse, the page keeps the last place it already has.

Times can differ by a few minutes from the timetable at your mosque. Use the mosque’s announcement when the two disagree. The Hijri date is calculated as well. Confirm the first and last day of Ramadan, and the two Eids, with your local community, because moon sighting can move those days.

Nasheeds and other reading

Nasheeds opens a library. Arabic is the list you see first. You can switch to Urdu before you play. The player is for listening. A refresh stops the sound, so a track does not keep playing after you leave the page.

Books holds seerah titles you can read on the site. The guides cover salah, wudu, ghusl, janazah, Hajj and Umrah, qurbani, and the prayer postures. Learn holds a short path, memorisation, tajweed, and stories of the prophets.

Scan Halal

Type a barcode, or scan one on a phone. The lookup reads the product name and ingredients from a public database, then checks those ingredients. Treat the result as a first look. Ingredient lists can be incomplete or out of date. A product with no ingredients is undecided. It is not a ruling that the product is allowed.

If you need a person

Daily Islam is a reading desk and a set of tools. It does not give a personal fatwa. For a question about the site itself, use the contact page. For who publishes it, see About.
